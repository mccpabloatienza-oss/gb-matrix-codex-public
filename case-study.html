Executive summary

Pablo Atienza, a marketing and business-intelligence professional in Argentina with more than 25 years of experience, directed the development of GB Matrix (Matriz GB) using ChatGPT. The original working artifact combines demand traction (ITD), inventory relevance (IRI), comparative model evaluation, backtesting, source reconciliation, interactive product analysis and separate SKU- and commercial-family-level views. The family-level perspective came from a human business decision: identifiers alone could not describe every meaningful family, so normalized titles, models and categories were incorporated alongside SKU evidence. Codex then developed an independent public demonstration with 800 entirely synthetic products, 12 periods, modular JavaScript, monthly CSV updates, 15 passing automated tests, a successful GitHub Actions pipeline and auditable development history. The proposed Content Path contribution is a monthly bilingual tutorial series showing how business experts can direct, verify and maintain software with Codex.

01. The artifact: from scattered signals to a decision system

Business problem

Sales, inventory, product variants and valuation data existed in separate sources. The original matrix connects these signals into demand-versus-inventory quadrants, interactive filters, product drill-down, tabular analysis and history, supporting decisions on which items to develop, review or sustain.

Methodological maturation

Alternative demand indicators and inventory-weighting approaches were reviewed and backtested. Source reconciliation, evidence quality and missing-value treatment remained part of the analytical system. The historical working artifact evolved through documented versions rather than one prompt.

02. Human reasoning made executable: SKU ≠ family

An SKU is an exact operational identifier, but it is not necessarily a commercial-family identifier. The author recognized two different real product contexts, anonymized as Brand 1 and Brand 2. One supported more structured identification; another required conservative matching across SKU clues, normalized product titles, models and categories. The original historical artifact acquired two complementary analytical perspectives: SKU-level traceability and family-level commercial intelligence. This was an implemented decision in the original private tool, not an invented vignette.

Attribution: the business expert defined the problem, tradeoffs and acceptance criteria. ChatGPT helped implement the original solution. The public Codex demo documents the family-identity insight but does not yet implement family grouping.

The audience is Spanish-speaking professionals in marketing, operations and commerce who know their businesses but do not have dedicated engineering teams.
